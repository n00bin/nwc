Two pools, not five effects

The game sorts these into two different buckets. Inside a bucket they add together; the two buckets then multiply.

Pool 1 — strip the boss’s defence

50% → 34.5% Defence

  • Armor Break−9.0
  • Midnight’s Malady−3.5
  • Faerie Fire−3.0
34.5 left standing15.5 stripped

A boss sitting at 50% Defence only lets 66.7% of your hit through. These three strip 15.5 points off that, down to 34.5% Defence — now 74.3% gets through. That jump is the +11.52%.

Defence divides your damage rather than subtracting from it, so each point you strip is worth slightly more than the one before it. That is why the three of them together beat the sum of what they each do alone.

The fine print

The boss these are measured against
Defence 50%, Deflect 50%, Deflect Severity 90%, Awareness 0, Accuracy 0.
The Awareness half of Midnight’s Malady is unverified
Awareness cancels out a player’s Combat Advantage bonus — your damage is × (1 + your CA − target Awareness). Every model we have puts bosses at 0 Awareness, in which case pushing it lower buys nothing and only the Defence half counts. But nobody has actually read a boss’s Awareness value in game, and whether it can be driven below zero is unknown. If it can, the debuff is worth roughly +1.6% more at the Combat Advantage cap, which would take the whole stack to +24.1%. The +22.1% here assumes it does nothing, which is the cautious reading. Either way it only matters while somebody is flanking.
Where the uptime figures come from
Proc uptime modelled as 1 − e^(−chance × events per second × duration). Armor Break lands near 98% at two hits per second; Faerie Fire only reaches about 45%, because it is a 10% chance on a three-second debuff. Weighting both pools by uptime gives +19.7% instead of +22.1%. These uptimes are a model, not a measured in-game number.
Spiteful Hex is the one that depends on the fight
It needs a critical strike to land on you, so its uptime tracks how hard you are being hit, not how fast you attack. Against a boss at 50% crit chance with a 70% proc and a 10-second debuff: one hit every 3s gives 69%, one hit every 2s gives 83%, one hit per second gives 97%. Holding a pack of adds pins it near 100%; a slow single boss does not. 85% is the middle figure used here. Even at the pessimistic end the full stack only falls from +22.1% to +20.6%, so the headline holds either way.
Capping Critical Avoidance does not switch Spiteful Hex off
Critical Avoidance cuts the severity of the crits you take, not how often they land — the validated formula is 1 + 0.50 × (0.90 − Critical Avoidance). At the 90% cap a crit stops hurting extra but still counts as a crit, so the proc still fires. What is not verified is whether a blocked hit still counts as being struck — a Paladin holding block may see fewer procs than this model assumes.
There is a second Faerie Fire
Blaspheme Assassin’s summoned buff carries the same name but does something different: boss Deflect −2% and +1% damage taken, worth +2.25%. That is slightly more than the racial version, and it is permanent instead of a 45%-uptime proc. Whether the two versions stack or overwrite each other is not verified.
Spiteful Hex also makes you harder to kill
Its self half is +4.5% Defence for 10s — but only if you are under the 90% Defence cap. At cap it is wasted. Faerie Fire’s −3% to enemy damage always counts. Together they are worth roughly 5% less damage taken.
What “the same as another DPS” means
Five players each doing one share of damage is five shares. Buff all of them by 22.1% and the group puts out 6.1 shares — the extra 1.1 is where the sixth-player comparison comes from. It assumes everyone contributes an equal share, which is a rough way to picture it rather than a literal measurement. The +22.1% itself holds no matter who is doing the damage.
